# Designing for the people who need this most

Legacy Protection is for everyone, but it is built first for the person most likely to be targeted by a scam or to lose the ability to manage their money: someone older, maybe with weaker eyesight, maybe not confident with technology, maybe reading in their second language. If it works well for them, it works for everyone else too.

## Words

- **Short sentences, everyday words.** We aim for a 6th-to-8th-grade reading level in English, and the equivalent in German and Spanish. A test measures it on every build (see "How we check" below).
- **Say what will happen, and what won't.** "Nothing happens unless…", "You can still stop it until…". No surprises.
- **Never rush and never guilt.** Scammers rush people, so we don't. Stopping is always the biggest, easiest button.
- **One word for one thing, everywhere.**

| We say | We don't say |
|---|---|
| your trusted person | trusted contact, guardian, nominee |
| "I'm okay" (check in) | check-in, heartbeat, liveness |
| your fingerprint, face or phone lock | passkey, WebAuthn, biometric |
| the person who will handle your affairs | executor (except in legal documents) |
| your money stays safe for your family | balance held for the estate |
| we paused it | blocked, flagged, declined |
| the messages that come with your payments | payment notes, memo field |

## Seeing and tapping

- Body text is at least 17–18px; headings are large and calm.
- Every button and link people tap is at least 48px tall, and main actions are 56px or more. The check-in button is 72px.
- Choices are shown, not hidden: radio buttons instead of drop-downs where there are four options or fewer.
- Money is accepted as "42.50" or "42,50", because half our readers write it with a comma.

## Language

- English, German and Spanish, the languages of PayPal's largest markets in North America and Europe. Each language is named in itself ("Deutsch", "Español") so someone who can't read the current page can still find theirs.
- The page arrives in the browser's language. A choice made with one tap is remembered on the device and on the account, so emails follow it.
- German uses the formal "Sie" and Spanish the formal "usted": what older readers expect from a money service.
- Dates, times and money follow the reader's conventions (`1.200,00 $` in German, `3:00 PM` vs `15:00`).
- Invitations are written in the inviter's language, because the person invited has no account yet and families usually share a language.

## How we check (instead of a native-speaker review on every change)

We don't have a native speaker reviewing every string, so the build checks what goes wrong most often with machine translation:

1. **Nothing dropped.** Every message is called with marked arguments; a translation that loses a name, amount, date or link fails the test. Invitation emails must keep their `{{link}}` placeholder.
2. **No English left behind**, and no server message reaches a German or Spanish reader in English (unknown messages become a plain "please check this part").
3. **Still fits.** A translation may be at most about twice as long as the English, so buttons and headings don't break.
4. **Still plain.** Readability is scored with the standard formula for each language: Flesch–Kincaid grade for English, Amstad reading ease for German, Fernández-Huerta for Spanish. No sentence may run past 32 words.
5. **Same facts.** The plan summary a person confirms is generated by code in each language from the validated plan; a property test checks that every language states every name, email and day count.
6. **Same protection (INV-25).** Scam phrases are recognised in all three languages, and the AI helper's "never tell them to send it" guard is checked in all three.
7. **Accessible.** An end-to-end run checks every main screen with axe-core against WCAG 2.2 AA, in German, Spanish and English, and checks tap-target sizes.

Results on 5 November 2026 (long-form messages only):

| Language | Score | Target |
|---|---|---|
| English | Flesch–Kincaid grade 3.8 | ≤ 8 |
| German | Amstad reading ease 73 | ≥ 55 |
| Spanish | Fernández-Huerta 84 | ≥ 65 |

Pages in German and Spanish carry a short line: "Translated with AI and checked automatically. If anything is unclear, please tell us." Before launch in a market, a native speaker who is also in our target age group should read every screen; the session script is in the testing plan.
